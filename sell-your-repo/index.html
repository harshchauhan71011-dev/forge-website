      A few things you might be wondering

      Fair questions.

      Who buys the code?

      AI research labs that train and test models on real, team-built software. The current buyer’s name is under NDA; you’ll always know the type of buyer before you sign anything.

      Why would anyone pay for old code?

      Public code has already been used by everyone. Private projects with years of real review history are scarce, and that history is what labs are paying for.

      I built it for a client. Can I sell it?

      Usually not. The client normally owns code an agency writes, and employment contracts usually assign it to the employer. We need written consent from whoever owns it, so tell us early.

      Why is the range so low?

      We can’t check self-reported answers, so the estimate is cautious on purpose. Once the real numbers are confirmed, the price usually goes up, not down.

      What if there are secrets in the history?

      Rotate them before anything else. Deleting a file doesn’t remove it from git history, and a repository with live credentials can’t be sold.

      Is it exclusive?

      Yes, for our current buyer. Once a repository is sold you can’t sell it again, and we check that the same repository isn’t offered twice.

      Do you need my GitHub login?

      No. Never to get a price, and never your password. If you decide to sell, you choose how we confirm the numbers, and you can revoke it.

      When do I get paid?

      After the buyer confirms they received the repository. We show you each step and a realistic date.

      How does Forge make money?

      We pay you, then sell rights to the buyer for more. The difference is our margin. We’d rather say it than have you guess.
